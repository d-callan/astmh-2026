The Scale Problem

  • Classical evolutionary analysis fits statistical models per dataset — trees, likelihoods, selection models.
  • That means hours to weeks of compute per analysis — and the cost grows with sequence count.
  • At archive scale — 10³ to 10⁵ haplotypes — that cost is prohibitive.
What surveillance needs

Answers on demand — not per-study heroics.

The problem
Keep it conceptual — no dN/dS math, no likelihood talk. The room cares about surveillance answers, not model mechanics: classical methods are per-dataset, per-study; surveillance is continuous.

The Idea: A Foundation Model
for Molecular Evolution

  • The same move as language models: train once on the evolutionary process itself — apply instantly to new sequences.
  • The model already carries what classical pipelines re-derive every run — so per-dataset compute drops to seconds to minutes.
  • And it doesn't need a tree: training showed pairwise distances suffice — so no heavy tree-building upstream, just a codon-aware alignment.
One sentence of honesty

It approximates the statistical answer — fast because the learning happened offline. It's in addition to classical tools, not instead of.

The idea
The analogy does the work: like a language model trained once on text, this is trained once on the evolutionary process. Bonus mechanism worth saying out loud: during training it became clear the model only needs pairwise distances (tn93 produces them), not tree topology — so there's no expensive tree-building step upstream of inference, just a codon-aware MSA. The honesty box preempts the inevitable skeptic question — encourage them to verify flagship claims on their own data; it's complementary to classical methods.

Scope — what's in vs out

In scope: protein-coding alignments — any pathogen or host gene within the ~200 My divergence window (a viral-tuned variant exists). Out: non-coding sequence.

Mechanism
The anti-magic slide. Input is just a codon alignment + TN93 distances. The axial transformer attends down the sites within each sequence, then across taxa at each site, with distances acting as positional encoding. Trained supervised on genes with precomputed classical (MEME-style) answers; validated against MEME — preprint reports rank concordance up to rho=0.983, ROC-AUC up to 0.94 (mean 0.66), >1,000x faster per locus and >10,000x at proteome scale. Scope: protein-coding alignments — any pathogen or host gene within the ~200 My divergence window; viral-tuned variant; not for non-coding DNA.

Two Instruments, One Model

HyphAeon — "the microscope"
Which sites, genes, lineages are adapting? Selection, convergence, mutational effects — the questions you ask about a vaccine target or a drug-resistance gene.
ChronAeon — "the radar"
Where does a new sequence sit on the tree? When and where did a lineage emerge? Streaming triage of raw FASTA — no alignment pipeline required.
The subtle point

Same underlying model — two sets of inference scripts aimed at different questions. Built by members of the BRC Analytics team — free and public on Galaxy, like everything else in this story.

HyphAeon + ChronAeon
Microscope = look closely at what's adapting (hyphaeon subcommands: meme/epistasis/dms/phenotype/splits/temporal/disease/filter). Radar = place and date sequences fast (chronaeon: triage/sketch/align/date/autoclock/dynamics/phylogeo). One trained model underneath both — different inference scripts for different question types.

Cadence caveat

Logan's index rebuilds every couple of years — this is retrospective power. Real-time surveillance = same machinery + a continuously-updated source.

Speed → scope
Two-tier honesty on runtimes: a typical study's analysis drops from hours/days to seconds/minutes; a whole-archive run goes from impossible to hours/days — don't quote numbers beyond that framing. (Preprint framing if pressed: <1 ms per codon site, >1,000x faster per locus than numerical CTMC likelihood, >10,000x at proteome scale.) And say the cadence caveat before they do: Logan's index is a snapshot rebuilt every couple of years, so this unlocks retrospective/historical studies — real-time surveillance would take the same machinery fed by a continuously-updated source (e.g. Pathoplexus).

Surveillance Without Annotation

  • The key property: nothing in this pipeline reads sample metadata.
  • A metagenome where the collector never suspected a pathogen still surfaces — if the sequence is there.
  • Mislabeled, pooled, or repurposed samples still contribute.
The consequence

Archived samples become prospectively useful — data collected for one purpose answers questions nobody thought to ask yet.

No annotation needed
This is the property that makes the pipeline surveillance rather than just search: hits don't depend on how the submitter labeled the sample — or whether they knew a pathogen was present at all.

The Bigger Claim

  • Retrospective surveillance at archive scale is now routine: find every sequence → rebuild haplotypes → place, date, and screen for adaptation — a pathogen's whole history, on public infrastructure, all recorded.
  • For tropical medicine: pathogens without dedicated sequencing programs — the archive is the cohort.
  • The forward-looking piece: this demonstrates the capacity for standing surveillance — the machinery scales. A live version needs a continuously-updated source (e.g. Pathoplexus), not new methods.
